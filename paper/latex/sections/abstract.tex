\textbf{Objective:} To describe carbon-plate insole use during preparation and competition among competitive Italian athletics athletes, and to estimate the cross-sectional, period-matched association between reported carbon share and self-reported current-season injury.

\textbf{Design:} Cross-sectional survey with two-phase (voluntary, then targeted-recall) recruitment, poststratified to national age--sex population strata; reported in line with STROBE and the STROBE Sports Injury and Illness Surveillance (STROBE-SIIS) extension.

\textbf{Participants:} FIDAL-affiliated athletics athletes (Under 20, Under 23, Senior categories) recruited nationally in 2026. Of 355 eligible, complete respondents, 352 (704 athlete-period records) formed the balanced analysis cohort.

\textbf{Exposure:} Carbon share, the proportion of reported weekly training-type frequency delivered in carbon-plated spikes across five training domains (long endurance, middle endurance, lactate, maximum velocity, technique), calculated separately for the preparation and competition periods.

\textbf{Outcome:} Self-reported injury occurring in the preparation period and/or the competition period of the current season.

\textbf{Statistical methods:} A Bayesian multilevel logistic model with period-specific intercepts, an athlete-level random intercept, a natural-spline exposure term among users, and adjustment for demographic, anthropometric, training-load, and discipline covariates. Estimands were posterior-standardized adjusted prevalences, differences, and ratios, reported with 95\% credible intervals (CrI) and posterior probabilities. Poststratification weights aligned the sample to national age--sex population estimates.

\textbf{Results:} Carbon-plated spikes accounted for a mean 35.7\% of reported preparation training and 47.7\% of reported competition training, a stable pattern across sex and age strata. Among 193 injured athletes completing four adapted OSTRC-H2 impact items, the median severity score was 75 (IQR 58--92) and was highest for injuries spanning both periods (84, IQR 67--92). Adoption of carbon use (median user versus non-user) showed no clear association with injury in either period. Among competition-period users, moving from the 25th to 75th percentile of carbon share was associated with a 14.3 percentage-point higher adjusted injury prevalence (95\% CrI 2.7 to 25.8; adjusted prevalence ratio 1.41, 95\% CrI 1.06 to 1.86; posterior probability of a positive association 99.0\%). The corresponding preparation-period dose contrast was unclear ($-0.8$ pp, 95\% CrI $-10.6$ to $9.1$).

\textbf{Conclusion:} Among competition users, a higher reported carbon share was associated with higher adjusted competition-period injury prevalence. Adoption itself and preparation-period dose were not clearly associated with injury. The cross-sectional, retrospective design does not support a causal interpretation.
